Whether it's a vacation or a business trip, you sometimes have to be away from home for several days or even weeks. During this time, your cat stays home and continues its life at its own pace... but how does it really react to your absence?

Can your cat forget you?
Contrary to popular belief that cats are solitary and independent animals, these little furballs are capable of forming strong emotional bonds with their humans. These bonds are built on trust, security, and familiarity. But what happens to this relationship after several weeks without you? According to feline behaviorist Hugues Martinat : "A prolonged absence can lead to some stress due to a new adjustment period, but it generally does not erase previously established emotional bonds."

Cats have episodic memory, allowing them to recall specific moments in their lives. They remember a visit to the vet or a particular experience, whether it was positive or stressful. So, if you've shared many days of cuddling, playing, and eating with your cat, it's very likely that they'll remember these moments and recognize your presence upon your return.

Their exceptional sense of smell also plays a key role in this ability. Some cats are even able to find their way home after several years of absence. This heightened sense allows them to detect your scent and identify you immediately, even after a long separation. It then becomes clear that the quality of time spent together is essential: these interactions nourish their well-being and strengthen your bond.

Hugues Martinat also points out that sleep takes up a large part of your cat 's day, around 15 to 17 hours a day. It's therefore not uncommon to find him in exactly the same place as in the morning when you leave for work. "Although he has a notion of the length of your absence, it is not perceived in the same way as you," he explains.

When your cat seems to be sulking...
Sometimes your cat may seem distant or "sulky" when you return. For Hugues Martinat, this is completely normal behavior. "By nature, cats are quite habitual creatures. A change in routine caneasily disturb them and result in a somewhat distant behavior."

Certain signs, such as vomiting or urinating, indicate stress or anxiety related to the situation. Your cat is developing coping mechanisms to deal with your absence, and your return may disrupt this recently established routine. That's why it's essential to remain patient and kind when you return.

Tips to make absence and return easier
Before you leave, it's recommended to maintain a calm and reassuring attitude. "It's important not to overdo the affection right before you leave," says the behaviorist. Cats appreciate routine, and consistency in their routine helps reduce their stress. So try to keep mealtimes, playtimes, and resttimes consistent.

When you return, pay attention to how your cat reacts. If he acts distant or sulky, let him come to you at his own pace. Offer him moments of pleasure, such as petting or treats, to strengthen the relationship and remind him that your presence is pleasant and secure.

Every cat is unique, and their reactions depend on their personality and experiences. Some will become more affectionate after your absence, while others will prefer a little distance. The key is to create a stable, safe, and warm environment so that your cat feels calm even in your absence.

Ultimately, even after several weeks without you, your cat will most likely remember you and the relationship you have built together. He may show his happiness in different ways, sometimes with cuddles, sometimes with distance, but always by remaining faithful to the precious bond you have established.
